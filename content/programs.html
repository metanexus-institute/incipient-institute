Programs

Research in practice.

Work at the Pavilion ranges from a Saturday conversation to a season of independent research. Some fellowships are renewed on the strength of work that continues after the fellow leaves.

Research residencies

Resident fellowships.

Each fellow receives a stipend, a desk, and eight weeks for independent work. Visiting fellows may stay on site in the Pavilion’s residential wing. Fellows share work in progress at the Open Table and contribute an entry to the public archive.

On-site lodging

Private rooms come with linens and access to a shared kitchen and laundry. Lodging is included in the fellowship. Keys are issued at the east desk, where staff can also arrange a step-free room.

Fellows may end a stay early by returning their key. The date a room is vacated and the date the fellowship record closes are entered separately.

The Room 3 extract shows what carries over to the next allocation. The east desk can provide a copy of a visitor record.

What fellows contribute

Each residency begins and ends with a note about the question that brought the fellow here. We publish both under the original title. The questions at the two ends of a residency sometimes have very little in common.

Community partnerships

Field grants.

Small grants pair independent researchers, artists, and practitioners with local organizations. Recent work includes a neighborhood seed archive and a listening project led by a local choir.

Recent field grant: The choir’s listening map

Choir members made recordings along different routes to rehearsal and marked where they could first hear the other singers. Twice, a recording picked up singing beyond the edge of the route sheet. The map now includes a second sheet for those locations.

Recent field grant: The unlit meadow

Local Pagan practitioners held seasonal vigils at the edge of the meadow. They asked that the path lights stay off until morning, both for the animals that feed there after dusk and for people learning to attend to a place by sound.

The hosts shared dates and notes on the grounds. They kept the vigils themselves private.

Project closeout

The choir asked to withdraw its recordings after members began hearing their own practice from rooms they had never rehearsed in. The files were removed. The listening map still includes those rooms.

A visitor learned one singer’s part by ear in the listening room. She sang it at the next rehearsal, taking a breath in the same place as the original singer. The original singer tried to come in with her and found she couldn’t remember how the part began. She asked for her recording back.

The archive invited the new singer to complete the final report. Her account is filed under the choir’s original project. See the annual return and the chair’s decision on the recording.

Saturday public program

The Open Table.

Each Saturday a fellow brings a question from their work. Visitors can listen or join the conversation. Mara plays five notes on the common-room piano when the table is ready.

The conversation meets at noon in the common room. If you’re on the meadow path, you can wait for the piano before coming in.
