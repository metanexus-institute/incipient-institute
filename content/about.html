About the Institute

A place to
think together.

Incipient began with scientists, artists, and philosophers who wanted to work together for longer than a conference weekend. The Institute supports research in complexity, the philosophy of process, and creative practice.

Our history.

From the first workshop, we’ve asked what an encounter makes possible. The campus records grew out of a practical question: what changes if the Institute itself becomes one of the things we pay attention to?

2009
Mara Vale and Jim Lyndon convene the first Incipient workshop in a borrowed library. The minutes include a contribution from the person who transcribed them the following year.
2014
The Pavilion opens with a common room, a research library, and six desks for visiting fellows. Lyndon chairs the founding board.
2018
Field grants begin. The Pavilion starts keeping the grounds survey, room plans, and fellows’ observations together.
2025
The Institute makes the first ten years of field notes available in its public archive.

Leadership and
senior fellows.

Mara Vale

Founder and Executive Director

A philosopher of process and former adult-education teacher, Mara founded Incipient to give independent scholars a place to work. She still convenes the Saturday Open Table.

Jim Lyndon

Co-founder and Chair of the Board

Jim works across technology, process philosophy, and institutional design. He co-founded Incipient to explore what becomes possible when people can change the institutions that bring them together.

Dev Raman

Senior Fellow, Complex Systems

Dev studies how small changes alter the behavior of a system. He leads the Pavilion survey and developed the Institute’s protocol for comparing observations made from different positions.

Lenora Bliss

Senior Fellow, Public Practice

An oral historian and sound artist, Lenora works with choirs and libraries to document how communities remember shared places. She directs the field grants and edits the archive.

Ari Reed

Director, Pavilion Operations

Ari trained in architectural conservation. They maintain the Pavilion, oversee visitor access, and have kept the building’s original drawings and daily records since it opened.

Requests to restore a room

In considering a request to return a room to its former use, the Board will give first consideration to the work now possible there. A trustee who recognizes the room as part of their own home should withdraw from the vote.

How we measure participation

Attendance records tell us who came. Follow-up records tell us how far the exchange went. An encounter counts when it causes a documented change. We attribute later changes to the original encounter, regardless of where they are reported.

Residents who leave early are included in the departure total. If their work continues to yield observations, it is also included among the year’s continuing fellowships. See the October residence record for an example.

The board now includes corrected accounts in the return. When an account changes what another visitor remembers, we record the change under the original visit. See the household notice and the chair’s note on revisions.
